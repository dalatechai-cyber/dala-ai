## The ceiling contradiction — RESOLVED 2026-09-01 (D-015)

**Resolved by the founder: Reception is sold against a band of 400 conversations/month.**
Kept here rather than deleted, because how it was found and how it was mis-stated are both
worth keeping.

### What it was

Two settled decisions were each correct alone and collided when combined:

| | |
|---|---|
| **D-004** derives the ceiling from the *discounted floor* | ₮200,000/mo at a 60% target margin → **$22.86/month** allowable model spend |
| **D-009** chose the model by measurement and native-speaker review | Sonnet 5, **~$0.0090 per reply** blended |

At the design's own A7 (6 messages/conversation), $22.86 buys **2,540 replies ≈ 423
conversations**. Reception was being sold with no stated limit against a ceiling that
affords 423.

### The resolution

The ceiling does not move and D-004's formula is untouched. **What was sold moves**: 400
conversations/month included, with ~5% headroom under the 423 the ceiling actually buys.
Overage is the existing degradation ladder, not an invoice — billing overage is undesigned
and must stay that way while no revenue path exists. Full reasoning in
[`DECISIONS.md` D-015](DECISIONS.md).

### Correction: I overstated what happens at the ceiling

An earlier revision of this section said Reception *"stops replying to Matrix's customers
around day 17 of every month. Not degrades: stops."* **That was wrong**, and wrong in the
direction of alarm. [`05-spend-ledger.md` §5.7](architecture/05-spend-ledger.md) designs a
five-state ladder, and state 2 keeps the salon meaningfully alive: deterministic shortcuts
still answer location, hours and greetings, canned refusals still answer, the closure
message still answers, and anything needing the model gets the Mongolian handoff line to
7741-7777 **once per conversation**.

So the real failure is *"for part of the month the AI receptionist becomes an address-and-
hours bot that tells customers to phone"* — a genuine product problem and not what a salon
paid for, but not silence. I asserted the stronger claim without reading §5.7, which is the
same mistake as the composition-table misses: **a claim about a document, made without
reading the document.**

### And the overshoot rests on a number nobody has measured

The 1.77× that forced this decision assumed **750 conversations/month** — a guess, and one
the design does not agree with itself about. `05-spend-ledger.md` assumes **300–600 at ~5
replies each**:

| Real volume | Monthly spend | vs the $22.86 ceiling |
|---:|---:|---|
| 300 conv | $16.20 | 0.71× — comfortably under |
| 420 conv | $22.68 | 0.99× — exactly at |
| 600 conv | $32.40 | 1.42× — over |
| 750 conv | $40.50 | 1.77× — over |

### Measured 2026-09-01 — Matrix was already inside the band (D-016)

Six days of the ancestor's production logs: mean **60.5 replies/day → ~1,842/month, 41%
of the assumed 4,500.** Spend **$16.57/mo, 0.73× the ceiling, 71% margin** — clearing the
60% target. A8 = 750 is refuted; `05-spend-ledger.md`'s 300–600 was closer.

**The margin result is solid** (it depends only on the measured reply count). **The
conversation count is not** — it still divides by the unmeasured A7 = 6. Count distinct
PSIDs instead; the log line carries them.

The band stays at 400: the busiest day measured (94), sustained, is ~477 conversations —
outside it. Six days carries weekly shape and no seasonal shape, so the mirror phase's 14
days remains the measurement that sets the final number.

#### 3.0 was not in the original plan, and it should have been

§6.7(b) calls an offline fixture of inflected forms "the cheapest correctness win in the
whole design and should be built in week one", and then the plan had no row for it. It is
now 3.0 because 3.3, 3.4 and the deterministic short-circuit all match Mongolian text and
would otherwise each have grown their own matcher.

The engine exists because the constructs one reaches for first are not merely imprecise
here — they are silently, systematically wrong. Verified by execution on this repository's
Node, and pinned as tests so they stay verified:

| | On Mongolian |
|---|---|
| `/\bзасалт\b/` | `false` |
| `/\w/` on `үс` | `false` |
| `'😊'.length` | `2`, so a length cap truncates mid-emoji |
| `Number('᠓')` | `NaN`, so a Mongolian-script digit reads as "not a number" |

That last one was found while writing 3.4 and is a hole an injected price could have used:
a numeral guard written the obvious way sees `᠓᠓,᠐᠐᠐` as containing no number at all.

The same execution settled why `unaccent` is *partially* destructive rather than merely
lossy, which CLAUDE.md rule 6 asserts without the mechanism: `Ё` and `Й` decompose under
NFD and `Ө` and `Ү` do not, so stripping combining marks flattens the two letters Russian
shares and leaves untouched the two that make the text Mongolian. It therefore works on
nine strings in ten.

**3.4 is done but uncalled.** The guard is complete and tested against 25 cases; nothing
invokes it yet, because the thing it guards — the model call — is 3.2. It is written first
on the same principle as Track 2: the reply is metered before it exists, and now it is
checked before it exists too.

#### 3.1 is done and deliberately unseeded

The compiler, the publish/rollback pointer moves, the review gate and the KB write-time
check are built. **`prompt/platform/` is empty**, so no platform block exists to compile
yet, and the gate refuses rather than rendering nothing.

That split is the point. Reviewing Mongolian prose and reviewing a build gate are
different jobs for different people, so the machinery merges on its own and the text
arrives as a change whose entire diff is text a native speaker can read. §6.6 asks for
exactly this and the draft did not have it: the `reviewed_at` gate covered *tenant* rows
while the platform's own Mongolian was covered only by code review, by people who do not
read Mongolian — and three errors had already survived into the draft's platform text,
the worst of them a duplicated verb in the one sentence whose job is to stop the model
following instructions pasted into a knowledge base.

`scripts/guards/check-mn-review.mjs` signs each block by **file hash**, so editing a block
after sign-off invalidates it. Proven to fail in all four directions before it was
committed — unsigned block, correct signature, edited-after-signing, and a stale signature
for a file that no longer exists — because a guard that has only ever been seen to pass is
not known to be a guard.

**Rollback re-renders nothing**, and that is a property rather than an optimisation: the
previous snapshot is still there byte-for-byte with the same `content_hash`, so the
prompt-cache entry it warmed is still valid. A rollback that recompiled could produce a
*different* prefix from the same rows if any input had drifted, which is the one thing a
rollback must never do.

#### ~~Open~~ SETTLED 2026-09-04: a reply may echo a numeral the customer wrote

The strict reading shipped first: `allowed_numbers` is compiled from the prompt, so a
customer asking «Маргааш 15:00 цагт болох уу?» got the pinned handoff line instead of an
answer, because 15:00 was not a compiled numeral.

**The founder reversed it.** A reply may echo a numeral the customer stated in their own
message when confirming or asking about that same value; the model still may not
*introduce* one that is in neither `allowed_numbers` nor the customer's text. The
distinction is between **confirming** a value already on the customer's screen and
**inventing** one.

Two properties keep the permission from becoming a hole:

- **The same tokenizer reads both sides.** The guard takes the customer's raw text, not a
  list somebody else extracted, and runs `extractNumerals` over it — so «7 000» from the
  customer and «7,000» from the model are the same number. A caller extracting under its
  own rule could split one into two and the echo would silently fail to match: a false
  refusal with no cause a reader could find.
- **The echo stops at Ш1.** Check 2b is passed an *empty* allow-list, so on a refused
  topic neither `allowed_numbers` nor the customer's own numerals satisfy it. A customer
  who writes «Хүүхдийн үс 33,000₮ мөн үү?» has supplied precisely the number that would
  make an echo read as confirmation of the thing the topic exists to refuse.

`customerText` is a **required** field on `OutboundContext`, not optional. A caller that
forgot it would silently get strict matching, and a false refusal is far harder to notice
than a compile error.

#### 3.11 — the freshness gate, and the file that had no tests

**`max_reply_age_minutes` = 30, settled by the founder 2026-09-04:** *"a bot answering an
hour-old Messenger message reads as broken, not helpful."* Per tenant, because §3.9 names
120 for GS Auto Center — whose customers are waiting on a car rather than a haircut — and
CLAUDE.md's test makes anything that distinguishes one customer from another a row.

The check sits **after the message is persisted** and before the reservation. Checking
first would be marginally cheaper and would lose the message; §3.4.5's rule is *persist
everything, generate nothing*, so the question survives and reaches the Quality layer as
one nobody answered. It costs three rows and saves a reservation, a paid call and a send.

`STALE_EVENT_HOURS = 20` stays. Two controls, two questions: is a reply still **wanted**
(30 min, per tenant, a product rule) versus still **deliverable** (20 h, platform, under
Messenger's 24-hour window). The tighter one now makes the backstop unreachable from the
worker, and it is kept anyway — `handleReception` is a library function whose guarantee
should not depend on which caller reaches it.

**And the worker route now has tests, because it stopped being the route.** Its twenty-odd
branches moved to `lib/worker/reception.ts`, which takes its effects as an argument and
returns `{ status, body }`; the route is the binding and may not branch at all. Five
mutations were each caught by exactly the test that should catch them: removing the
freshness gate, letting a `shadow` channel deliver, turning an unreadable event into a 200,
turning an indeterminate send into a 503, and skipping the signature check.

Two defects the harness found immediately:

- `flagQuality` omitted `tenant_id`, which is NOT NULL and part of the composite FK. Every
  stub accepted it; PostgREST would have rejected the first real write.
- The first test fixture wrapped the payload in `{object, entry:[…]}`. `raw_payload` holds
  **one entry** — the webhook stores a row per entry because one POST can carry two
  tenants — so the fixture extracted zero messages and the job returned a cheerful 200
  `nothing_to_answer`. A wrong fixture reads exactly like a quiet success.

#### 3.2 is done and deliberately unwired

The request builder, the failure classifier, the timeout and the stale-event check are
built and tested. **Nothing calls them.** The worker still releases its reservation
without spending, exactly as Track 2 left it, and it stays that way until the platform
blocks exist — because a compiler with no blocks has no prompt to send.

Three request properties are not stylistic, and each was checked against the current API
contract rather than written from memory:

- **`thinking: { type: 'disabled' }`.** Sonnet 5 accepts it and **rejects `budget_tokens`
  with a 400**, so pinning thinking off is the only way to stop it eating the reply budget
  (D-014). There is no "just cap the thinking" option to fall back on.
- **`tools` is absent, not an empty array.** The single largest reduction in what a
  successful injection can achieve: there is no tool to call.
- **No `temperature`, `top_p` or `top_k`.** These are *removed* on Sonnet 5 and return a
  400. Porting the ancestor's `temperature` would have failed every request — a check
  worth making before the first call rather than after it.

**`stop_reason` is read before `content`.** A safety refusal arrives as HTTP 200, so code
that reaches for `content[0].text` first sees a plausible body and mislabels a terminal
condition as a normal reply. That is precisely how the ancestor turns one refusal into
four paid calls.

##### A pre-existing §6.2.6 violation, found by building the guard

§6.10.5 asks for a CI grep for date-suffixed model ids. Writing it surfaced something
else: `scripts/bakeoff/run.mjs` carried its own copies of both model ids and both price
tables. Two registries, in two languages, free to diverge — which is the duplication
§6.2.6 forbids, spelled differently enough that nobody noticed.

Fixed by making `config/models.json` the one registry that both the TypeScript app and
the `.mjs` harness read. Exempting the harness would have been easier and would have left
the second copy in place.

The database already had half of this check — `catalog.sql` V12 asserts no date-suffixed
id in `model_prices`. The new guard covers the source, which V12 cannot see.

#### 3.7 was not in the plan, and the plan cannot work without it

Wiring the route revealed it. The worker's job carries `{ eventId, tenantId, conversationId }`
and the flow needs the customer's message text and the last ten turns — **and nothing in
V1 writes them.** The webhook stores `webhook_events.raw_payload` and stops; there is no
`persons`, `contacts`, `conversations` or `messages` row created anywhere.

Every row of Track 3 assumed the text was simply available. None of them says who puts it
there. That is the third gap of this exact shape found in this session — after 3.0's
matching engine and the six missing `canned_response_kinds` — and all three share a cause:
**a plan written as a list of capabilities does not notice the plumbing between them.**

It is the reason 3.6 lands uncalled. The flow is built and tested against stubs; the route
cannot call it until something has written a message row for it to read.

#### 3.9 — two bills that are not errors

Nothing throws, no request fails, no status code changes. **The only symptom is the
invoice, a month later.** That is the entire reason these need alarms rather than logs.

| | What it costs | Why nothing catches it |
|---|---|---|
| Caching silently stops | ~**2.5×** the bill at the design's P = 9,000 | The replies are still correct |
| A model silently swaps | The Mongolian quality changes | Nothing visible changes |

##### The window is read from the ledger, not from a counter

`spend_ledger` already records `cache_read_tokens` on every call and is append-only,
enforced by `ENABLE ALWAYS` triggers that bind `service_role` too. So the rolling window is
a query over evidence that **cannot have been rewritten**, and it needs no new table.

Three thresholds, each chosen against a specific false alarm:

- **Ten consecutive cold calls**, not one. The first request after a TTL expiry always
  writes rather than reads, so alarming on one would fire constantly and train the channel
  to be ignored — which is the failure mode the alert dedup exists to prevent in the first
  place.
- **Below ten it is `insufficient_data`, not "warm".** Reporting health from three samples
  is a false reassurance; reporting the alarm is a false positive.
- **A tenant with caching off never alarms.** That is a choice, not a regression.

##### Exact comparison, on purpose

`checkServedModel` does no normalising, no prefix matching, no "close enough". The whole
value of the check is noticing a difference we did not intend, and any leniency is a rule
about which differences do not matter — a judgement that belongs to a person reading an
alert, not to a string comparison.

##### The dedup keys differ, and the difference is the point

`cache_cold` and `model_swap` carry the **day**, so a still-broken cache re-announces
itself once daily — the right cadence for something costing money continuously. `model_not_found`
carries **no period**: it is a single event that stays true until somebody edits the
registry, and repeating it daily would add noise to an outage rather than information.

##### And the guard got an escape hatch rather than a weakened test

`check-model-ids` flagged a date-suffixed id in the *test* that proves a dated id is
**detected** — the exact opposite of pinning one, which a grep cannot tell apart. The
tempting fixes were both bad: weaken the test, or concatenate the string to slip past the
grep. Instead the guard gained a `// not-a-pin:` marker, the same escape hatch
`check-cyrillic-matchers` already has, and it is **line-scoped** — a marker on the
preceding line does not exempt the next one. Proven both ways.

#### 3.8 — the largest saving in the design, and it was unreachable

§6.3.8 prices the replies this absorbs — where the model is paid to retype a
`canned_responses` row — at **₮26,300/tenant-month**. It could not be built, because
`deterministic_replies` is `(tenant_id, intent, body, enabled)`: **a sentence with no way
to decide when to send it.** §6.8 specifies `match_mode`, `stems` and
`requires_empty_history`; none of them existed.

Fourth gap of this shape in the session, after 3.0's matching engine, the six missing
`canned_response_kinds`, and 3.7's whole persistence layer. `0005` adds the three columns,
every default in the safe direction, plus `enabled_rule_can_match` — because a row that
reads as live on a dashboard and can never fire is invisible, which is the failure this
repository keeps finding.

##### The stem engine does NOT fix the ancestor's location bug — the mode does

Worth pinning, because it is easy to assume otherwise. `хаяг` is four characters and
appears at a token start in «Facebook хаяг байна уу», so `contains_stem` fires and the
address shortcut steals a question about the Facebook page — **the ancestor's live defect,
reproduced by a matcher working exactly as designed.**

What fixes it is whole-message matching: the customer did not ask "where are you". That is
why `0005` defaults `match_mode` to `whole_message` and why §6.8 says it is the only mode a
greeting may use. A test asserts both halves.

##### When in doubt, do not fire — the mirror image of the gate matcher

The two matchers look alike and their failure directions are opposite:

| | Skipping a malformed rule means | So on doubt |
|---|---|---|
| `gate/match.ts` | a refusal the tenant asked for stops firing, and the topic becomes discussable | **refuse the whole match** |
| `gate/deterministic.ts` | one message costs a model call it could have avoided | **skip the rule** |

A short-circuit that fires wrongly refuses a paying customer with no model in the loop to
recover; one that fails to fire costs ₮39 and the model answers perfectly.

##### Unknown history is not empty history

`HistoryState` is `{known:true, empty:boolean} | {known:false}` rather than a boolean.
The ancestor's `getHistory` returns `null` when Redis is unreachable and `[]` only when it
genuinely answered "no turns"; collapsing them makes a storage hiccup greet an existing
customer from scratch, mid-conversation, as though the last ten minutes had not happened.

##### And it runs AFTER the review gate

A deterministic reply is still a customer-visible sentence. An unreviewed one must not ship
just because no model was involved in choosing it.

#### 3.7 is done, and the worker is wired end to end

`webhook_events.raw_payload` → extract → contact → conversation → message → chokepoint →
flow → an `outbound_messages` row in `draft`. The send is still absent, and that is the
right place to stop: the draft row is the durable hand-off, and the send path claims from
it.

##### The PSID is keyed, not merely hashed

`person_identities.value_hash` carries the schema comment "a PSID is a stable person
identifier, i.e. PII". A plain SHA-256 does not honour that: a Meta PSID is a ~16-digit
number, so the space is 10^16 and a fast hash is brute-forceable on commodity hardware —
anyone with the table could recover every PSID and rejoin them to Facebook profiles.

It is an HMAC under `IDENTITY_PEPPER`. That does not make the value secret; it makes the
hash **unusable without the key**, which is the property the column exists for.

##### Four things the extractor must skip, and one that would be expensive

| Skipped | Why |
|---|---|
| `is_echo` | **Meta delivers our own outbound messages back to us.** Answering them is a loop that bills. The single most expensive thing that can go wrong in that file |
| `delivery` / `read` | Status events with no message; treating one as a turn puts an empty message in front of the model |
| attachment with no text | A sticker carries no question, so a reply would be invented from nothing |
| `postback` | A different product surface with its own routing, and V1 does not ship one |

Every skip is **reported**, never silently dropped — "we saw it and chose not to answer" is
a different fact from "it vanished".

##### One reservation per reply, not per delivery

A Meta entry may carry several messages, and each gets its own trip through the chokepoint.
Reserving once and answering three times would be a ceiling that counts wrong in the
tenant's favour. History is read *after* the inbound row is stored, so the turn just
received is not also passed as history — the model would otherwise see the question twice.

##### The conversation window is a pricing decision

`CONVERSATION_IDLE_MS` is 24 hours, matching Messenger's own window and the ancestor's
Redis TTL so the three agree rather than drift. **It is also the definition of the unit
Reception is sold in**: D-015's 400-conversation band counts these rows, so a shorter
window sells fewer conversations for the same traffic and a longer one sells more. Changing
it is a pricing change wearing a code change's clothes.

##### Still missing in L4, stated rather than hidden

§6.4.2 wants the volatile tail to carry open/closed-now and the active closure sentence
quoted verbatim from a row. Both need `business_hours` and `tenant_closures`, which nothing
reads yet. What is in L4 today — the tenant-local time and the channel — is correct; what
is missing is missing.

##### And a guard that was wrong in a way twelve pull requests never hit

`check-supabase-nostore` matched `/import\s+([\s\S]*?)\s*from\s+['"]@supabase\/supabase-js['"]/`,
which starts at the **first** `import` in the file and runs to the supabase one. In every
file so far the supabase import happened to be first, so the captured clause was exactly
the import clause. In the first file where it was not, the clause became
`{ createHmac } from 'node:crypto'; import type { SupabaseClient }` and the type-only test
failed on it — a false positive.

The tempting cure is to reorder the imports until the guard is happy, which would leave the
defect in place for the next file. Fixed at the regex (`[^;]*?` cannot cross a statement
terminator) and proven three ways: the real file passes, a genuine value import still
fails, and the inline `{ type X }` form is still allowed.

#### 3.6 — the flow, and why every branch is testable

`handleReception` takes its inputs and does not fetch them: the route loads, it decides.
That split is what makes each of the eighteen branches a question about *what to do*,
unentangled from *how to find out*.

The order is the design again — **three refusals come before the model call and each ends
the request for free**: a stale event, an unparseable matcher, an unreviewed canned line.
The cheapest refusals are first and none costs a token.

Two decisions worth naming:

- **A retryable failure leaves the reservation in `called`.** We do not know whether tokens
  were consumed; releasing would give back a hold that may have been spent. The expiry
  sweep reconciles it. An unknown is parked, not guessed — the same posture as
  `indeterminate` on the outbound side.
- **A ledger failure does not refuse the customer's reply.** The money is already spent and
  they are owed the answer, so it alerts and drafts anyway. `ledger_deadletter` exists for
  exactly this.

##### Two config values the design names and the schema lacks

`tenant.primary_script` (§6.10.3) and `upstream_timeout_ms` (§6.10.4) are both read by the
design and neither is a column. Handled without a migration:

- **`primary_script` is derived from `default_locale`.** It *is* derivable, and a second
  column holding the same fact is a second thing that can be wrong. Russian and Mongolian
  both resolve to Cyrillic, which is right — the check asks "is this reply in the
  customer's writing system", not "which language is it".
- **`upstream_timeout_ms` is a compiled constant.** No tenant needs a different value, and
  a nullable column nobody sets is a config surface that only confuses. The read site takes
  it as a parameter, so the day one is needed the constant becomes its default.

##### And one guard check that was unreachable

`forbidden_phrasings` has a single `phrase` column and no gate, so §6.7 item 7 — the
per-gate forbidden-vocabulary check, which the section calls "the prompt rule **and** the
production detector" — could never fire. The loader was passing an empty map: a check
built, tested, merged and unreachable.

`0004` adds `gate` and `stems`, both nullable with a CHECK that they are set together. The
nullability is deliberate: a row may exist purely to record evidence (`rationale`,
`observed_at`, `evidence` are the table's other half), and a phrasing observed but not yet
reduced to stems is worth keeping. The loader skips those rather than flattening them into
some other gate's list — which is precisely the failure §6.7(a) describes.

#### 3.3 — the matcher is built; the words are drafted and unsigned

The inbound matcher and the «БЭЛЭН ХАРИУЛТ» section are code and are done. The ten blocks
are drafted in `prompt/drafts/`, which **nothing loads**: `check-mn-review.mjs` scans
`prompt/platform/` only, so a draft cannot reach a customer by being forgotten.

**A match does not silence the model.** §10 is precise: matchers run inbound *only to
select which gate text is rendered*, and outbound over our own text for the guard. Only an
explicitly opted-in `deterministic_shortcircuit` answers with no model call, and it is off
per topic per tenant until a precision run says otherwise — a short-circuit that fires
wrongly refuses a paying customer with no model in the loop to recover.

**A malformed matcher refuses the whole match.** Skipping it would mean the topic the
tenant explicitly asked never to be discussed becomes discussable, with nothing anywhere
going red. Stems shorter than four characters are refused for the same reason in
miniature: `үс` (hair) also fires on «үсэрсэн», «үсрэх», «үснээс».

##### Drafting the blocks found a gap nothing else would have

The ten blocks name nine canned-response keys. **Only one of them was seeded.**

`canned_response_kinds` was written when the gate had six checks. Ш0 and Ш9 were *added*
under review, Ш6 was *promoted* out of the abuse check into a standalone always-evaluated
one, and Ш4/Ш5 were only ever described as "out of scope" in prose. Nothing connected the
two documents, so four checks had no row to put their sentence in — and two more would have
had to share one.

Sharing would have been the subtler bug. `canned_responses` is keyed
`(tenant_id, kind, locale)`: **one kind means one sentence.** Ш4 ("I do not have anyone's
schedule") and Ш5 ("I am not a doctor") are both "something we cannot know", so both map to
`refusal_out_of_scope` if you go by description — and the tenant then answers a
pregnancy-safety question with a line about staff rosters. A kind is not a category; it is
the primary key of a sentence.

`0003_gate_canned_kinds.sql` adds the six missing kinds (additive only), and
`check-gate-keys.mjs` now fails the build when a block names a key no migration seeds — so
the next Ш-block added under review cannot repeat this.

That guard's own first version read **8 of the 16** seeded kinds, because
`('budget_exhausted_notice', 'Ceiling reached; hand off to the phone')` puts a semicolon
inside a description string and the block regex ended there. It reported six real keys as
missing. Bounded by statement now, and worth recording: a guard that silently under-reads
its own source produces false failures here and would produce false passes in the mirror
case.

#### 3.5, the half that needs nobody's permission

The claim and lease are built; **the send is not**, because it needs a decrypted per-tenant
Meta token and no KEK exists.

The done-test is the whole reason this half exists. QStash guarantees **at-least-once**
delivery of the job; Messenger needs **at-most-once** delivery of the reply. Those
contradict each other unless something turns "run this job again" into "finish sending the
message we already wrote" — which is what the draft row does. Without it a redelivery
re-enters generation: a second paid Anthropic call, a second pass through the boundary gate
that may answer differently, and two different replies to one customer question.

Uniqueness is the partial index `(tenant_id, kind, dedup_key)`, not a check-then-insert:
two workers racing on the same redelivery both attempt the insert, one wins on `23505`, and
the loser reads the winner's row. A read-then-decide-then-write lets both through. The
claim is likewise one conditional UPDATE with the state CAS and the lease check **inside
the WHERE clause**.

**`indeterminate` is a seventh state and it earns its place.** A send that timed out
*after the request left* may have been delivered. Retrying replies to the customer twice;
marking it failed loses a reply that may never have arrived. So it is neither — it is
parked, `indeterminate` is not claimable, and no automatic retry can pick it up. The
ancestor already learned this one level up (`messenger.js:118-133` deliberately does not
retry a timed-out enqueue while it does retry a hard-failed one), and it is exactly the
distinction a rewrite loses.

### Track 4 — tenant #1 (week 5–6)

Phases C, E, F, G of the onboarding checklist, then D (mirror), H (shadow), I (cutover),
J (handover). The mirror phase is what makes the cutover cheap: 14 days of real inbound
timestamps, zero risk, no subscription change — and it is also what sets
`prompt_cache_mode` from measurement instead of a guess.

## Which onboarding steps V1 skips

The checklist is ~41 numbered steps across ten phases. V1 executes all of Phases A, C,
D, E, F, G, H, I, J. It **skips or defers** these:

| Step area | V1 |
|---|---|
| Instagram channel binding, IG-Login rehearsal | **Skipped.** `auth_flavour='instagram_login'` stays out of the constraint until a step rehearses it |
| Comment-field subscription and enrichment | **Skipped.** DM-only, which also narrows App Review |

| `handoff_targets` + Telegram `/start` with the salon | **Not skipped.** Without it the handoff path has no destination |

**Two steps are load-bearing and easy to skip under deadline pressure, and both are

without a published config, and cannot go `active` without a passing probe run. If the

## The rule this document is defending

Anything not in the table above does not go into V1 **even when it is a good idea**,
